Fly it with no network at all.

A laptop, a 915 MHz radio and an autonomous quadcopter. No internet, no cellular, no ground control software.

System architecture

915MHz Long-range radio link LoRa, validated on hardware
0 Networks required No internet, cell or ground station
18min Hover endurance Holybro figure, stock airframe
1.5kg Payload capacity Holybro figure, stock airframe

The two endurance and payload figures are Holybro's for the stock X500 V2, not measurements of this build, which carries a Jetson Orin Nano and an OAK-D Pro. Everything shown below is built and bench-tested unless a caption says otherwise. Outdoor flight is pending.

Hardware

Hardware Design

A faithful rendering of the drone's hardware architecture, derived directly from the physical drone into CAD data.

Pixhawk 6X

Flight controller on the top plate. FMUv6X is the open autopilot hardware standard it is built to.

NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano & LoRa radio

The companion computer, under the payload shelf, with the 915 MHz radio on the shelf edge beside it. It runs the command server and talks MAVLink, the standard autopilot protocol, to the 6X. The radio is a Heltec Wireless Stick V3; the geometry here was exported as the Lite, so the render is missing its small OLED.

OAK-D Pro

Stereo depth camera on the nose bracket, with an infrared dot projector and flood illumination.

Holybro M10

GPS on the mast at the tail. Fitted, and getting a fix.

2216 motor and propeller

One of four. Holybro's CAD names it DJ-2216-KV880; the kit listing says KV920. Unresolved, so neither number is claimed here.

Carbon arm and clamp

One of four, on a 500 mm wheelbase. The tube is held in a moulded two-piece clamp where it meets the body plate.
