Mission

Built to keep people safe

Three real problems at UTSA. One autonomous drone.

01No app. No signal. Still connected. Live video and text on any phone.

The drone hosts its own Wi-Fi and server. Any phone can connect and watch live video in a web browser, with no app to install. It also sends text messages over its long-range radio to every station in range, like a cell network without the towers.

02It finds the worker who needs help. Watches the crew, ignores everything else.

It patrols a UTSA construction site on its own and checks on the crew. Onboard AI locks onto workers in high-visibility vests, ignores everything else, and sends an alert when someone may need help.

03No one walks to their car alone. An escort from above, at night.

Campus safety can send the drone instead of an officer. It flies to the person who asked, follows 3 meters behind them to their car, and watches from above. If the radio link drops, it flies home on its own.

Hardware

Built from proven parts

Rendered from the manufacturers’ 3D models and our own board designs. The two circuit boards, the propellers and the radio housing were designed for this project.

Power distribution board

Designed for this project. It carries the 4S battery to the four ESCs and makes the 12 V rail for the Jetson and the 5 V and 3.3 V rails for servos and sensors. Shown without its solder mask, so the copper shows.

Handheld remote

Designed for this project. A Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3 with its screen, a knob and a back button, on its own board. It runs the motor tests and a 4 ft hover demo over the 915 MHz link, with no laptop. Shown without its antenna.

Pixhawk 6X

The flight controller, mounted on the top plate. Built to FMUv6X, the open autopilot standard.

NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano & LoRa radio

The onboard computer. It runs the AI and the command server and tells the flight controller what to do. The 915 MHz radio sits on the shelf above it: a Heltec Wireless Stick V3, shown here as the Lite model, without its small screen.

OAK-D Pro

The eyes. A stereo depth camera that measures the distance to everything it sees, with infrared lighting for low light.

Holybro M10

GPS, mounted high on the tail mast for a clear view of the sky.

2216 motor and propeller

One of four brushless motors, each turning a 10-inch propeller.

Carbon arm and clamp

One of four carbon fiber arms on a 500 mm frame, each locked into the body by a two-piece clamp.
